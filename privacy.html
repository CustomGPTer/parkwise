Privacy policy

Last updated 23 November 2026. This policy covers the Parkwise app for Android (package uk.jj.parkwise) and this website. Parkwise is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom, who can be reached by opening an issue on the project's GitHub page.

The short version

  • Where you park, your photos, places and settings are kept on your phone. They are never uploaded to the developer or to anyone else.
  • There are no accounts, no adverts, no analytics and no tracking.
  • The only things fetched from the internet are map pictures.
  • When you share a spot, it goes only where you send it.

What stays on your phone

Parkwise keeps these in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read:

  • Your parkings: the position, time, name, notes, level, bay, zone, ticket time and cost.
  • Photos you take in Parkwise. They are kept inside the app, not in your gallery, and their location details are removed.
  • Your places, vehicles and settings.
  • Map areas you save for use with no signal.

Android's cloud backup and device transfer are switched off for Parkwise, so none of this is copied to a Google account. If you make a backup file, it is locked with your password (AES-256) before it leaves the app, and it goes only where you choose to put it, such as your Google Drive.

Maps

The map is drawn from OpenStreetMap data, downloaded as map squares from OpenFreeMap, which needs no key and no account. If you switch on satellite view, pictures come from Esri. Like any website, those servers receive the address of each square your phone asks for, and your internet address. They do not receive your saved parkings, and Parkwise sends them nothing else.

Sharing

When you tap Share, Parkwise writes a plain message with the spot's coordinates and a Google Maps link, and hands it to the app you pick, such as WhatsApp or Messages. Opening Google Maps or Waze for directions hands them the car's position. Parkwise has no server, so nothing passes through the developer.

Reading signs

Reading a car park sign is done on your phone by the open-source Tesseract engine. The photo is never uploaded.
